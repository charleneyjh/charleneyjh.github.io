Business opportunity

Twilio is a developer platform for telecommunications. It bridges the gap between telephony and internet. Growth team’s mission is to activate, retain and grow self-service customers to become sales-qualified-leads (SQL).

 

Our previous analysis has shown that PA10 (10 API calls from a customer) customers are 5x more likely to become an SQL. Our team uses PA10 as a proxy for successful user onboarding/activation. However, our PA10 rate across all products and nations are only at 13%, which means that 87% of Twilio signups don't even use our products. 

Target customers

New Self-service customers who are trying to build their first application with Twilio, and evaluate if Twilio is the communication vendor they should use.

From the audit, I identified the following potential user pain points on Account Dashboard:

  • No clear guidance on what users should do

  • Multiple CTAs compete for attention

Meanwhile, there are 60+ variants of Account Dashboard that are desperate or out-of-date. It has became a burden for the Growth team to maintain and iterate. 

User research

To further understand the customer journey and identified the pain points, I conducted 13 interviews with self-service developers across regions with various coding levels.

 

The research objectives were:

  • Identify customers’ Jobs-To-Be-Done with Proof of Concept (POC) and productionizing with Twilio SMS

  • Identify the key levers for success and major roadblocks in user onboarding

  • Find out what resources on Account Dashboard help with onboarding

Top findings:

​Insight 1: Upon creating a Twilio account, users want to quickly build out a Proof-of-Concept to evaluate Twilio. They expect a clear starting point on Account Dashboard.

The current Account Dashboard doesn’t provide the needed guidance. Users often use resources outside of Twilio Console to onboard themselves.

Insight 2: Users expect a contextual and personalized experience, including relevant code samples and applicable information the helps them evaluate if Twilio can meet their needs.

The current “one size fits all” experience doesn’t align with users expectations.

Based on the interviews, I was able to map out the Jobs-to-be-done (JTBD) with building out a Proof of Concept (POC) and productionzing with Twilio SMS, and the helpful resources or roadblocks within each step. This has became the foundation of our onboarding design concept.

Customer problem statement

As a self-service developer who wants to use SMS, when I’m trying Twilio for the first time, I want to quickly evaluate Twilio for my use case and build my first application.

Currently, I filled out the onboarding questionnaire, but are not served with relevant information catered to my use cases and coding level. I fumble around different sources like Google search, blog posts, and Twilio docs that seem relevant to my use case, which may or may not lead to success. I feel frustrated and overwhelmed.

Product Goal

Establish an onboarding framework that aligns with new customers’ Jobs-To-Be-Done, and is applicable and scalable to all Twilio pillar products.

Design challenge
Design challenge

1. Balance Twilio onbording and product-based onboarding

From interviews, I've discovered that there are task-based product onboarding and general education around Twilio. When is the best time to introduce the right information that aligns with customers’ JTBD, and set them up for success without overwhelming them?

2. Establish a framework that other team can leverage

What are some onboarding themes that can be generalized? How might we provide the onboarding framework and guidance that could benefit all the pillar products and uplevel the UX of the entire Twilio platform?

3. Design a scalable and flexible Account Dashboard that's useful throughout the customer journey

With Account Dashboard being the landing page for all Twilio customers, although our team's current focus is on onboarding, I need to be considerate of other user needs throughout their life cycle, and accommodate those in my design.

Design iteration

What we've heard

  • Unclear navigation. Users were confused around whether they should proceed with clicking “Next step”, or answer “Yes” to our feedback question, or clicking the primary CTA within each step.

​

  • Unnecessary complexity. Introducing Twilio Functions (A severless solution that powers the sample app) at this point introduce unnecessary cognitive load and might get the users off track.

Improvement

  • Steamline navigation
    Take out the feedback system question while keeping the easy reach to common issues FAQ. Iterate on copy to reduce navigation confusion.

​

  • Reduce complexity
    Do not introduce the concept of Twilio Functions here. Instead, present a demo video.

What we've heard

​While more novice users appreciate the hand-holding approach, power users would like to go striaght to docs. We need  much clearer navigation to provide a way for users to skip certain steps and jump straight to where they need to.

The onboarding guide

Step 1. Get a Twilio phone number

Get a phone number is the first step to send an SMS with Twilio. To further reduce friction, our team built out the API to auto-provision a US phone number with one-click.

Most countries outside of US required regulatory approval to get a local phone number. The design calls out this information to set the right expectation for international customers, while encouraging them to proceed with a US number for testing. 

Step 2. Send your first SMS

We embed a code editor with curl to send their first SMS through Terminal, and incorporate a feedback system to ensure user is supported.

To further streamline the experience, we pre-filled the Twilio credentials, "From" number and "To" number.  

Happy path

It's a big deal to light up your phone for the first time. We celebrate user's success and introduce Messaging Insights, a powerful analytics tool to monitor their app.

Non-happy path

If user is not able to receive an SMS from Twilio, we surface the most common issues and point them to Twilio SMS Support page to troubleshoot.

Step 3. See an app demo

We point users to a relevant sample app based on the use case they fill out on the onboarding questionnaire. 

​

Users are able to try out the demo app live, and copy and code that's used in the app for their own use.

​

They also have the option to watch an app demo video. 

Step 4. Follow the tutorial

We point users to the Twilio docs site with a tutorial to learn how to send and receive Twilio SMS line-by-line.

Step 5. Launch and scale

We surface relevant guides and document on scaling, account management, pricing and regulation policies. 

​

From customer interviews, these resources have proven to be key in the evaluating Twilio as a communication vendor.

Result and impact 

The new onboarding experience has been live for about a month. 85-90% of the customers who ​have seen this new flow successfully sent their first message with Twilio, compared to 65-70%  in our legacy design. We also observed a 14% lift in PA 10 (making 10 API calls) and upgrade metric. This is the biggest improvement in our funnel since the Growth team founded. Other teams outside of SMS, Voice and Verify expressed strong interest in using our onboarding platform.
